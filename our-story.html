Our Story

Why "Rahmah"?

As an organization supporting Muslim women and mothers, Rahmah (رحمة) Collective earned its name because of the extraordinary depth contained within its Arabic roots.

The word raḥmah is derived from the triliteral root ر-ح-م (R-H-M), a root that appears hundreds of times throughout the Qur'an in words associated with mercy, compassion, tenderness, and loving care. From this same root come some of Allah's most beautiful Names: al-Rahman (ٱلرَّحْمَٰن), The Universally Merciful, and al-Raheem (ٱلرَّحِيم), The Especially Merciful.

This root gives us another beautiful word as well, Ra'him (رَحِم): the womb.

This is not merely a linguistic coincidence. Our Holy Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) taught us that Allah says in a Hadith e Qudsi:

"I am al-Rahman, and I created the Ra'him (womb), deriving its name from My Name."

The Arabic language itself teaches us that mercy and the womb are inseparably connected. Classical scholars describe Rahmah as a mercy that is active rather than passive; it is not simply pity, but a compassion that nurtures, protects, sustains, and allows life to flourish. The womb embodies this ethos more perfectly than perhaps any other creation: it shelters without being asked, nourishes without expectation, and gives of itself completely so that another may live.

For an organization dedicated to women's health, maternal well-being, and the sacred journey from preconception through motherhood, we could think of no name more befitting.

Rahmah reminds us that care is not merely clinical, but that it is rooted in compassion. It is informed by knowledge. It is guided by dignity. And it ultimately reflects a Divine attribute that Allah Himself chose to linguistically connect with the very place where every human life begins.

Our name is therefore both our identity and our aspiration: to embody rahmah in every resource we create, every family we serve, and every woman we support.
